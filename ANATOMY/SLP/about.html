Anatomy study: evidence and limitations

Open-source surface anatomy — not clinically validated. The optional 3D view loads 20 groups of source-derived BodyParts3D 4.3 larynx/trachea meshes, not primitive shapes. A human anatomy specialist has NOT approved their use as examination or clinical models. The complete true vocal-fold mucosal layers, laryngeal nerves and fine tissue architecture are absent. Do not use for diagnosis, surgery, clinical simulation, competency evaluation, or 3D printing.

What is supported by references?

The viewer's original written descriptions and 12 formative practice questions cite the educational works below. Those citations support the written teaching notes, not the geometry. The independent BodyParts3D mesh source and license are documented separately below.

  • OpenStax, Anatomy & Physiology 2e, §22.1 — Organs and Structures of the Respiratory System
  • NCBI Bookshelf, Anatomy, Head and Neck: Larynx
  • NCBI Bookshelf, Anatomy, Head and Neck: Laryngeal Muscles

Clinical terminology, muscle actions and teaching prompts require independent review by a qualified anatomy or speech-language professional before this becomes an authoritative educational atlas.

Real 3D model provenance, transformations and license

The 3D model is a curated adaptation of BodyParts3D / Anatomography 4.3, credited to The Database Center for Life Science (DBCLS), via the OrganUI Larynx open anatomy project. Its mesh data remains CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan (attribution and share-alike required). The MIT license for upstream software does not relicense these meshes. Neither DBCLS nor OrganUI endorses this viewer.

OrganUI assembled 45 BodyParts3D 4.3 meshes into 20 groups and converted them to a Y-up GLB, recentering and rescaling coordinates and assigning colors; no decimation or invented anatomy is reported. We distribute the GLB unchanged and display it alongside original study content. The site build downloads the model from a pinned upstream commit and verifies both the 5,257,920-byte file size and its SHA-256 digest before packaging it.

Model digest: d07598a36e0177c0a1c87c28df145500d3e1766a9f02b58d003bd85e775202df.

Limits: The source has the paired vocal ligaments, not whole, mucosa-covered true vocal folds. Paired muscles and cartilages are grouped together and cannot prove which side is individually selected. Models omit mucosa, most nerves and vessels, and many other laryngeal tissues. Their color is illustrative and clinical accuracy review is pending.

New: source-derived 3D lungs, trachea and bronchial trees

The separate 3D Respiratory Anatomy Lab uses an unchanged binary sourced from OrganUI Lungs, pinned at upstream commit 5815084f43efe438ebfae3fd889ee2baf343dfbf. It is a converted, simplified subset of BodyParts3D / Anatomography 4.3, © Database Center for Life Science (DBCLS), adapted by OrganUI in 2026 and offered under CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan, independently of application source-code licenses. See exact source provenance and runtime attribution notice.

The source GLB contains 17 lung parenchymal segment groups, the trachea, and one bronchial-tree group per side (20 selectable source groups), reconstructed from 119 original source elements. The pinned build checks its 3,901,260 bytes and SHA-256 b2b19e5f7473036598e3552a5835a4ebc0f55c786ddd00863cb6a170409ed346. Exposed lung-segment boundaries are approximate source surfaces, not a clinical pulmonary segmentation reference. No lungs were fabricated with generic ellipsoids; the new whole-curriculum 3D constellation is an unrelated abstract navigation diagram, never anatomical geometry.

What remains absent from the 3D respiratory model: diaphragm, ribs, intercostals, pleura, pulmonary vessels, alveolar detail and some fine branches. The existing text/2D respiratory lesson covers some of these separately, without claiming 3D representation. Neither the static lung meshes nor the larynx mesh simulates actual breathing or phonation, and their original coordinate frames are not assumed interchangeable. These are separate viewers rather than an artificially aligned whole-body reconstruction. Model license/source attribution and educational-science accuracy still need human review.

Orientation and missing structures

The schematic uses the subject's left and right, not the viewer's. In an anterior-facing view the subject's left appears to the viewer's right. Rotation changes screen positions; rely on structure labels instead of screen location. The shapes and their alignment are still unvalidated.

The sourced mesh includes the epiglottis, hyoid, trachea and several intrinsic muscle groups. It still omits full vocal-fold mucosa, false vocal-fold coverings, many nerves, vessels, and tissue layers. Missing data does not mean those structures are absent from a person.

Learning activities and assessment limits

This draft uses four explicit learning objectives and 12 formative short-answer prompts with evidence links and explanations. Self-ratings are only for prioritizing another review in the current session. They are not grades, calibrated mastery measurements, or delayed spaced repetition. No study history is saved.

Retrieval practice is supported by a systematic review of health-professions education research, but this particular anatomy tool has not been studied for educational effectiveness.

Privacy, accessibility and licensing

No personal coursework, patient records or proprietary medical meshes are included in the public viewer. The review questions do not store scores. WebGL failure leaves text-based learning usable. Automated desktop and emulated-phone checks do not replace real-device or assistive-technology evaluation.

The BodyParts3D 4.3 runtime model has pinned source and license records. Any future additions still require file-by-file permission, source, version and attribution review. This work's 3D model remains under CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan regardless of the MIT code license upstream.

Release status

This is a draft prototype, not a clinical tool. Anatomy accuracy review, genuine 3D model provenance, physical-device usability, and integration with the private H.E.R.B.I.E. educational library remain incomplete.
