Diaphragm, breathing, and speech

  1. Breathing in: During inspiration, the diaphragm contracts and moves downward, increasing thoracic volume and allowing air to enter.
  2. Quiet breathing out: Diaphragm relaxation and elastic recoil help move air out of the lungs.
  3. Speaking: Respiratory control supports the subglottal airflow and pressure used for phonation. The static diaphragm geometry does not display muscle activation or speech pressure.

Study question: Which structures in this model depict the primary inspiratory muscle? Identify both hemidiaphragms, then use Focus selected or Isolate selected to compare them.
