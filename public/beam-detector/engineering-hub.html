Our nine engineering tracks

01 — Visible light tripwire

Owned: red/blue LEDs, Fluke, resistors, 9V snaps, 2N4401/2N4403, wire. Next: verify emitter resistor; measure DC mV light/blocked/restored; prove NPN switch. Missing: independently proven light-signal amplifier/comparator and stable threshold.

02 — Motion prank

Phone camera software possible. Standalone PIR detector not confirmed. Quiet visual trigger is a separate build option.

03 — Pop-up surprise box

Start with cardboard, a lightweight spring and latch. Prove 20 safe jam-free mechanical cycles before adding motors.

04 — Object-removal alarm

Use salvaged mouse microswitch contacts and a current-limited LED. First map COM/NC using Fluke continuity with power disconnected; then test 20 cycles.

05 — Wireless clicker

433MHz-labeled fob available. Compatible documented RF receiver and decoder not confirmed. Don't guess command codes.

06 — Mouse-powered gadgets

Map microswitches and salvage wheel/housing first. The imaging chip is not a drop-in hallway receiver.

07 — Standalone silent control box

Build a low-voltage manual switch-to-indicator stage. Reuse proven NPN switching, with power and current calculations. No buzzer for now.

08 — Invisible IR tripwire

Amazon remote may emit coded IR bursts, but matched continuously suitable IR emitter/receiver hardware is not identified. Verify wavelength, timing, eye safety and interface.

09 — Moving/talking robot

Diagnose donor power and motor specifications before selecting controller, driver, protected supply and end stops. Movement first, sound optional.

Everything required — interactive checklist

Use the parts and electrical test worksheet to inspect actual component readiness for all nine projects, estimate LED current and record ten genuine DC optical readings. Results are user-entered, not verified automatically.

Every build must pass

  1. Confirm specific part numbers and source ratings.
  2. Disconnect power and check component polarity, resistance, continuity and pin orientation.
  3. Use a resistor in series with each powered loose LED; do not feed 9V into an unknown PCB.
  4. Prove one subsystem at a time; measure current using DC volts across a known resistor.
  5. Complete actual switching with no manual intervention (where required).
  6. Log repeated crossings, misses, duplicate counts and stationary false alarms.
  7. Mount securely, strain-relieve wiring and retest in different lighting.
