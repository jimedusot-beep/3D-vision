2. Prove automatic detection with the flashlight

  1. Open the hosted detector directly in Safari. Confirm V4 · Build + Test; refresh if an older version appears. Allow camera access and tap Start camera.
  2. Select White flashlight. Set the phone and light about one foot apart with the rear camera facing the source. Hold both stationary.
  3. Tap the light spot inside the preview to center the yellow target. Keep the source on; wait a moment, then tap ARM AUTOMATIC DETECTION once. Do not cover the light while arming.
  4. Cover the optical path with a card for about one second, then uncover for about one second. The status should change DETECTED → BROKEN → DETECTED and count one interruption. Do not touch the phone during crossings.
  5. Use the range test controls for ten crossings followed by a two-minute stationary check. If it misses a crossing or counts twice, record a failure and improve alignment before repeating.

The automatic detector uses a fixed light-on reference, hysteresis and about 120 ms of sustained low readings. Very short crossings can be missed; measure quick crossings separately. It cannot identify whether a hand, a switched-off emitter, movement or changed room light caused the loss. Camera exposure changes can still affect readings.

3. Measure before wiring

  1. With no cells installed, inspect the holder: cell size, contacts, damaged insulation and switch. Do not assume wire colors or a round three-cell holder prove series 4.5 V wiring. Check switch continuity with power removed if needed.
  2. Measure the resistor out of circuit on Ω. A common 330 Ω ±5% four-band resistor is orange–orange–brown–gold (about 313.5–346.5 Ω). Other band counts differ; meter reading controls.
  3. Inspect the LED. An intact longer lead usually indicates anode (+); a flat package edge often indicates cathode (−). Trimmed legs can defeat those clues. Use diode-test mode with power disconnected; record polarity and measured forward drop if the meter can forward-bias it. A failed diode-mode reading alone does not prove a dead LED.
  4. Install only three matching 1.5 V alkaline cells of the holder's correct format. Set the meter to DC volts, leads in COM and V/Ω. Measure output and label the actual positive and negative wires. A minus sign means the probes are reversed. Verify the switch really turns output on/off.
  5. The circuit below assumes roughly 4.5 V and a standard low-power red LED. If the wiring or supply cannot be verified, or output exceeds 5 V, return to the intact flashlight. Do not substitute 3.7 V lithium cells.

4. Connect one LED in series

Power off: battery + → resistor → LED anode → LED cathode → battery −. Clip securely and insulate every exposed junction. The resistor can sit on either side of the LED as long as it is in series.

Example only: at 4.5 V and a 2.0 V LED forward drop, 330 Ω gives about 7.6 mA; resistor dissipation is about 0.019 W. These are calculated values, not measurements of your parts. An unidentified LED has no verified current rating. A known 1 kΩ resistor lowers the example to about 2.5 mA for an initial check; use 330 Ω only after checking that the LED rating supports the calculated current. Never bypass the resistor to gain distance.

Measure current without changing meter jacks: keep the meter on DC volts. With the circuit powered, measure voltage across the resistor. Divide by its measured resistance: I(mA) = 1000 × V(resistor) / R(Ω). For example, 2.50 V across 330 Ω means 7.58 mA. Record supply, LED drop, resistor drop and calculated current. Do not put a meter in current or resistance mode across a battery. Disconnect immediately if wiring, cells or LED heat, flicker unexpectedly or smell unusual.

5. Align and increase range

  1. Switch the app to Red LED mode. Start 6–12 inches away; aim directly at the camera and tap the red spot. If an overexposed red LED looks white, try White flashlight mode and record which mode you used.
  2. Arm again after changing light mode, aim, lens, distance or room lighting. Camera stops, backgrounding and stale video invalidate an active test.
  3. Work through 6 inches, 1 foot, 3 feet, 6 feet, 10 feet and 15 feet. At each distance make ten crossings and then a two-minute clear-beam test.
  4. If signal is weak, move closer, shade background glare and stabilize alignment. A broad LED may fail at longer distances. A mouse lens is optional and unproven; it was designed for short-range sensing.
  5. Keep camera and emitter below faces and away from direct eye exposure. Put any short dark shade around the receiving view without covering the lens or contacting the phone glass.

6. Use the built-in qualification runner

  1. Measure distance and select source, room lighting and duration. Start with 2 minutes; choose 5 minutes for final qualification. Arm and wait for BEAM DETECTED.
  2. Tap Start ten-crossing test. Cross exactly ten times without touching the screen. For initial trials block about one second and fully restore between attempts.
  3. After completing ten actual crossings and restoring the light, check the confirmation and tap Finish crossings. The app compares the camera count with your report. A mismatch or blocked beam fails the trial.
  4. Leave the beam uninterrupted until the timer ends. Any detected interruption fails the stationary check. Do not adjust the camera or light during the run.
  5. Download the JSON result. Completed attempts retain source, mode, lighting, distance, arming reference, camera description, elapsed stationary time, operator confirmation and outcome. The latest 50 attempts save locally when storage is available. There is no cloud sync or image recording.

The app measures light loss; you supply crossing ground truth. A checked box is an operator statement, not independent proof. The range summary groups passes by source and lighting; inspect individual records before treating a configuration as qualified.

7. Mount and finish the doorway trial

  1. Secure both ends at knee-to-waist height for the game, with clear passage and no cables across walking space. Keep a separate stable battery transmitter on one side and the phone on the other. Verify nothing can tip.
  2. Run ten deliberate crossings, then try slower, quicker and partial crossings; record misses and extra counts separately. Shorter than roughly 0.2 seconds is a separate, unqualified capability.
  3. Repeat in daylight, artificial lighting and a dimmer room. Re-arm for each setup. Record measured range rather than assuming 15 feet.
  4. At the chosen operating distance, pass ten crossings, a five-minute uninterrupted run, and beam restoration. Stop and restart the camera; re-arm and repeat the ten-crossing check to verify recovery.

Version 1 passes only at the distance and lighting you actually tested. Passing at 6 feet is a six-foot prototype; 15 feet remains a separate milestone. The phone must stay on, unlocked and foregrounded. Screen wake lock is optional browser support, so check the screen during setup.

8. Stronger fixed-rig validation

The newer owner field report calls for 30 crossings and a 15-minute uninterrupted check at the fixed 15-foot setup. Select 30 crossings and the 15-minute stationary check in the guided runner. Tally each physical crossing against the displayed count, confirm that there were no misses or extra counts, then leave the beam clear for the timer. Record hardware, lighting and recovery in the test sheet. Do not treat a ten-crossing pass as the stronger qualification.

Does not detect blockingBlock between source and lens; ensure background light is not dominating. Use full one-second blocks first.
Multiple counts per crossingRestore continuously between trials. Remove moving glare and check loose wiring. Repeat and retain the failed attempt.
