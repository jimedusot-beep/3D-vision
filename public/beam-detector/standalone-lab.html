BUILD THE RECEIVER

Step-by-step engineering plan using your photographed salvage parts. The blue LED shines; an unpowered red LED senses; transistors can drive a separate warning LED—but an automatic sensor interface is still unverified.

What actually works?

Demonstrated by owner report: iPhone camera automatically detects the red-light beam; user reported 1, 3, 6, 10 and 15 ft passes. Loose red LED receiver showed some response to blue light on the Fluke, but calibrated DC on/off values and repeatability are not logged.

Not built yet: a phone-free receiver that independently detects the blue beam and illuminates a warning LED. The Fluke is a measurement instrument, not the final receiver.

How the whole system will work

Don't wire the red sensor LED straight into the transistor's base. A few millivolts is not enough to reliably turn a 2N4401 on. The future amplification/threshold circuit is the critical missing engineering step.

9-stage hands-on roadmap

Open a stage, follow its steps, run its check, then choose owner-reported pass, failed, or blocked. Progress is not pre-filled.

0 · Prepare a safe test benchNOT TESTED
  1. Move the components off bedding to a dry hard tabletop, with clean lighting.
  2. Disconnect 9V snap(s) while arranging all wires. Keep bare battery terminals from touching metal.
  3. Separate transistor legs; attach insulated short wire extensions using wraps and tape. Prevent any touching leads. Use the few clips only where helpful.
  4. With battery disconnected, measure a 1 kΩ resistor and a 10 kΩ resistor using the meter's Ω setting. Record the measured values.
  5. Never connect the 9V supply directly to a bare LED or unfinished NightFire board.

Pass when: the workspace is stable, connections insulated, and resistors verified.

1 · Confirm blue sender power is safeNOT TESTED

Correct series path for a typical indicator LED on 9V: 9V + → measured 1 kΩ (¼W or higher) → blue LED anode (+) → cathode (−) → 9V −.

With the battery disconnected, trace the actual blue LED wiring. Confirm the resistor is electrically in series, not simply nearby on the NightFire board. Do not power the board's other pads. Insulate each splice.

Measure actual voltage across the series resistor using DC V while powered; calculated current in mA = 1000 × resistor voltage (V) / resistor Ω. Turn off immediately if anything heats, smells, or shorts.

Pass when: the blue LED lights steadily with a verified resistor and no unsafe wiring.

2 · Test the unpowered LED receiverNOT TESTED
  1. Choose a spare red LED, different from the powered red warning LED. Connect its two legs only to Fluke COM and V/Ω probes.
  2. Choose DC mV, not AC mV. When probe tips touch, reading should settle near 0 mV.
  3. Aim the powered blue LED at the red receiver from 1–3 inches away. Read voltage with light ON.
  4. Block the blue path fully with cardboard; record BLOCKED. Restore the path and record RESTORED, without moving probes.
  5. Repeat ten times and look for a repeatable, correctly timed difference. More voltage isn't automatically better: consistent contrast matters.

Do not connect this detector LED to any battery yet. A drifting reading or a single AC mV change is not a valid pass.

Pass when: ten consecutive blocking cycles produce distinguishable DC responses.

3 · Shield and quantify the light sensorNOT TESTED
  1. Place the unpowered red LED inside a dark, short cardboard tube to reduce stray light; leave probe clips insulated and outside.
  2. Compare dark and illuminated DC mV levels at 1 inch, 3 inches, 6 inches, then 12 inches.
  3. Try room lights on/off without touching the sensor. Record variation and whether a hand crossing the beam is more reliable than changing overall room brightness.
  4. If there is no stable voltage contrast, stop here and investigate a real phototransistor, LDR, or amplifier instead of pretending the receiver is ready.

Pass when: a repeatable distance and stable ambient conditions are documented.

4 · Verify the 2N4401 transistor's pinsNOT TESTED
  1. Read marking 2N4401. Leave the 2N4403 (PNP) aside for now.
  2. Do NOT guess left/middle/right from a generated image. TO-92 2N4401 pin diagrams vary among suppliers. Get the exact maker/pinout or check with Fluke diode mode.
  3. For an NPN junction test, the red probe on the base should show a forward diode drop to the other two legs with the black probe, typically around 0.5–0.8 V; reversing usually reads open. This identifies the base, not always emitter versus collector.
  4. Confirm E, B, C with authoritative pin data and label three insulated wire extensions accordingly.

Pass when: emitter, base and collector are defensibly identified, including their source or test method. If not, mark BLOCKED and do not power the circuit.

5 · Test the 2N4401 as a switch, without solderNOT TESTED

Only after verifying its pinout: use a separate visible LED, 9V snap, measured 1 kΩ, measured 10 kΩ, insulated wires and a temporary touch point.

  1. Power OFF. Battery negative → E. Battery positive → 1 kΩ resistor → warning LED anode. Warning LED cathode → C.
  2. Attach a 10 kΩ resistor to B. Leave its opposite end free and insulated from everything else.
  3. Connect 9V snap. Briefly touch the free 10 kΩ resistor end to 9V positive, then remove it. Don't touch battery positive to B without that resistor.
  4. LED should illuminate while touched, and go dark when released. If it stays on, try a verified 100 kΩ resistor from B to E, then retest. Disconnect battery before changing anything.

Pass when: three consecutive ON/OFF cycles are achieved with no shorts or overheating. This proves the switching stage only, NOT optical detection.

6 · Design and verify the missing light-signal interfaceNOT TESTED

Critical blocker: the unpowered LED's few millivolts cannot directly drive the 2N4401 base. We need a genuinely responsive, biased light sensor plus high-impedance amplification and a threshold/switching stage. Nothing in your photos proves that such a complete interface is present.

  1. Identify exact part numbers in the remaining #16 chip bag, especially a real comparator, op-amp or phototransistor. A generic 2N4401 transistor by itself is not a millivolt comparator.
  2. Pick a topology only after we have real sensor light/dark measurements and actual supply voltage. Use manufacturer pinouts, not inferred PCB holes.
  3. Prototype at inches. Adjust threshold so the warning light is OFF with strong beam and ON only while blocked; repeat the test with ambient light changes.
  4. If the needed interface part is missing or the sensor isn't sensitive enough, mark BLOCKED. Don't apply 9V directly to the unpowered sensor LED or guessed IC pins.

Pass when: the warning LED automatically responds to actual beam interruption with zero hand-operated switches, at a repeatable close range.

7 · Separate transmitter and receiver, then range testNOT TESTED
  1. Keep the sender and receiver on separate low-voltage setups. Confirm no shared wire is needed across the hallway.
  2. Hold each in a dark tube or small rigid housing; keep electrical joints insulated and tubes away from moving feet.
  3. Measure 3 in, 6 in, 1 ft, 3 ft, 6 ft, 10 ft, then 15 ft, only if the previous distance works.
  4. At each distance perform 10 beam blocks with full restoration; note missed and duplicate warning flashes. Also leave the light unblocked for two minutes to check false positives.
  5. Record the first distance that fails. Your phone's 15-ft result does not transfer to this new receiver.

Pass when: the target distance is repeatable with no manual control and its limitations recorded.

8 · Finish quiet obstacle-course versionNOT TESTED
  1. Secure the receiver and emitter mounts on a stable, nonconductive base at a safe low height. Keep wiring out of the walking path and LEDs away from eyes.
  2. After mounting, recheck voltage, resistor temperatures, polarity, alignment and current; no exposed energized conductors.
  3. For a fixed-rig qualification, aim for 30/30 actual crossings plus 15 minutes with no false trips; record ambient conditions and battery voltage before/after.
  4. Repeat after battery replacement and moving/replacing the mounts. Ensure interrupted beam turns on warning light automatically.

Pass when: the physical unit works reliably without phone, laptop, sound or touching a button for each interruption.

Things that can stop progress

  • 9V straight to LED: disconnect and add a verified current-limiting series resistor.
  • Wrong transistor leg: mark the stage blocked; verify actual manufacturer pinout instead of guessing.
  • Fluke number drifts: short the probes to confirm near 0 DC mV, then check clips, ambient light and stable light/dark cycling.
  • Transistor switch passes but optical signal does nothing: expected until a working amplifier and threshold circuit exists.
  • Cylinder isn't a glowing beam: a dark tube reduces stray rays; it doesn't make a visible line in ordinary air or increase energy.
  • Long-distance failure: improve sensitivity and optics, not LED current beyond its unknown rating.
